# How to Write a Data Management and Sharing (DMS) Plan

If you're a researcher seeking NIH funding, you're likely aware that you need to prepare and submit a detailed Data Management and Sharing (DMS) Plan along with your funding application. In this page, we'll highlight key components of this DMS Plan.

Please note that this isn't an exhaustive description of what's needed for NIH. For detailed instructions, we encourage you to refer to the [application guide](https://grants.nih.gov/grants-process/write-application/how-to-apply-application-guide), as well as requirements outlined in your funding opportunity.

## What is a DMS Plan?

The DMS Plan reflects [NIH's 2023 DMS Policy](https://grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/sharing-policies/dms/policy-overview). With this policy, NIH is looking to significantly expand data sharing as one of the key research products, ensuring that the research community has timely access to NIH-funded scientific data. For general information on NIH policy expectations for sharing research data, visit the [Scientific Data Sharing](https://grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/sharing-policies) website.

## When is a DMS Plan Required?

The policy pertains to you if:

1. you're applying for NIH funding (e.g., through a new application or a competitive renewal of grant, contract, or other transactions), or
2. you're an intramural scientist at NIH funded to generate or work with data.

### What Qualifies as Scientific Data?

The 2023 DMS Policy [defines scientific data](https://grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/sharing-policies/dms/policy-overview#research-covered-by-the-2023-data-management-&-sharing-policy) as recorded, factual information that's useful for replicating research findings. Those data may or may not be part of a scholarly publication.

### Other Considerations

In addition to the 2023 DMS Policy, there may be other policies that apply to your work. For example, if you're conducting large-scale genomic research or small-scale genomic research of program priority and rare disease nature, the GDS policy applies to you and therefore your DMS Plan should include key elements described in the NIH [Genomics Data Sharing (GDS) Policy](https://grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/sharing-policies/gds/overview) (e.g., type of genomic assay, number of subjects and timeline for submission to an NIH-designated repository, estimated data release timeline, etc.). NIH's GDS Policy applies to all NIH-funded research (e.g., grants, contracts, intramural research, regardless of funding level) that generates or reuses genomic data from large-scale human or non-human research. While a separate GDS plan is no longer required, how to manage genomic data subject to the GDS policy becomes part of the DMS plan as a data type. Even if your genomic data is not subject to the GDS policy, you should still address how to manage and share the data in the plan that adopt best practices (e.g., de-identification of data to protect privacy, use data standards).

## Why Do I Need a DMS Plan?

If you're seeking NIH funding, you're required to submit a DMS Plan to comply with the current policy.

But you're not simply checking off a box in your NIH applications. By enabling others to leverage your data quickly and easily, you're ensuring that everyone in the cancer community can reap the benefit of your work in the same way that you would benefit from theirs.

Moreover, if you take time now (before your research begins) to think through your DMS processes, you can facilitate funding decisions, avoid costly delays and help repositories plan for timing and resources. You'll have your data work done!

## What Do I Need to Address in the Plan?

The initial implementation of the 2023 DMS policy required applicants to plan for their DMS by addressing six key elements. Most recently, NIH applications due on or after May 25, 2026 are required to use the [2026 format page](https://grants.nih.gov/sites/default/files/dms-blank-format-page-2026.docx) for DMS plans. The goal is to reduce researchers' administrative burden while retaining the spirit of the DMS policy. NIH recognizes that science is constantly evolving and you may not be able to anticipate all data types/datasets to be generated from your research proposal at the time of writing funding applications. However, you still need to plan how to manage the data you will generate and share, so any key information necessary for meaningful DMS should be provided to the extent possible. For example, you can describe the estimated number of subjects/participants in your study (one time point versus longitudinal), what types of data that you expect to generate, manage, preserve, and share, when and how, and justify for any data you won't be sharing (e.g., any restrictions imposed by federal, Tribal, or state laws, regulations, or policies). For additional guidance, see "[Writing a Data Management and Sharing Plan](https://grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/sharing-policies/dms/writing-dms-plan)."

Mock DMS plans serve as examples to illustrate how to use the new template. These examples are not intended to be replicated verbatim nor guarantee approval of your DMS plans that use similar technologies and generate similar data types.

## What Does NCI ODS Recommend?

NCI's Office of Data Sharing recommends that you work with program staff, librarians, institutional data cores, and other experts to plan for DMS. Ensure your DMS plans:

- Include data types/datasets as described in your application's Research Strategy section to create consistency between the research proposal and the DMS Plan, which will also make progress reporting easier.
- Share your data following the FAIR (i.e., findable, accessible, interoperable, and reusable) Principles. Include any necessary digital object identifiers, accession numbers, and hyperlinks to data sets whenever possible.
- Select established repository(ies) with infrastructure and procedures in place for data security, use of data standards, and long-term preservation capability to share your data. Repository selection may depend on your program expectations, domain-specific data types, or other requirements, including options such as the [Generalist Repositories](https://grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/sharing-policies/accessing-data/scientific#generalist-repositories), one or more of [NCI's Cancer Research Data Commons (CRDC) repositories](https://datacommons.cancer.gov/resources), such as [GDC](https://gdc.cancer.gov/), [PDC](https://pdc.cancer.gov/pdc/), [GC](https://general.datacommons.cancer.gov/), [ICDC](https://caninecommons.cancer.gov/), etc., that would require a committee review and approval.
- Determine if GDS policy applies to your research.
- Set realistic timelines for preserving and sharing your data. Be sure to consider tasks that could alter your data sharing timeline (e.g., repository policies, award record retention requirements, journal publication schedules). If you perform genomic research subject to the GDS policy in the DMS Plan, be sure that you obtain an [Institutional Certification](https://grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/sharing-policies/gds/institutional-certifications) by Just-In-Time (JIT) and the [data submission and release timelines](https://grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/sharing-policies/gds/data-submission-and-release-expectations) align with policy expectations.

## DMS Plan Resources, Tools, and Initiatives

Now that you have a sense of what your plan should contain, use the following resources to find additional information for refining your DMS Plan.

### Resources and Tools

- [DMS Plan](https://grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/sharing-policies/dms/writing-dms-plan): Learn what NIH expects in a DMS Plan and get additional direction on developing a plan.
- Repositories: Visit our [Index of NCI Studies](https://studycatalog.cancer.gov/) for a list of data collections produced by major NCI initiatives and other widely used data sets. Also, [visit the NIH Scientific Data Sharing website](https://grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/sharing-policies/accessing-data/scientific) for a full list of NIH-supported repositories.
- [Training Modules](https://www.nigms.nih.gov/training/pages/clearinghouse-for-training-modules-to-enhance-data-reproducibility.aspx): Watch these training modules on how to enhance data reproducibility.
- [Writing Applicable Activity Codes](https://grants.nih.gov/sites/default/files/flmngr/List-of-Activity-Codes-Applicable-to-DMS-Policy.pdf): Review this list of activity codes to see if you need to write a DMS Plan for your research.
